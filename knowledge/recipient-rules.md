# Recipient Rules

## Faculty and supervisors

- Respectful and collaborative.
- Familiar faculty do not need ceremonial formality.
- Make the request easy to answer.
- When reporting progress, state confirmed facts and the one remaining question.
- Avoid wording that sounds like assigning work downward to a teacher.
- For internal coordination, explain why information is needed when that affects willingness to help.

## Students

- Plain, direct, easy to understand.
- State process, deadline, required information, and consequence explicitly.
- Do not use literary or heavily bureaucratic wording.
- If the student is responsible for tracking a process, say so clearly and explain what they need to do.
- Do not hide a hard rule behind vague politeness.

## External institutions and hospitals

- Tactful, balanced, precise.
- State what the school has confirmed and what still needs the institution's confirmation.
- Avoid sounding as though the institution is being ordered to comply.
- When documents or contracts are involved, preserve exact document names, copies, signatures/seals, attachments, fees, and sequence.
- If correcting a discrepancy, describe the discrepancy neutrally before asking for clarification or correction.

## Vendors and service providers

- Friendly but practical.
- Explain school/payment/procurement constraints as process facts, not accusations.
- Confirm what has been received and state the next administrative step.
- Keep the message short unless a specification needs detail.

## Colleagues

- Efficient, collaborative, and natural.
- Avoid overexplaining background they already know.
- For group scheduling, state the reason briefly and ask the concrete availability question.

## Alumni / event participants

- Warm and welcoming.
- Keep event essentials prominent.
- For posters and forms, prioritize readability and space.
