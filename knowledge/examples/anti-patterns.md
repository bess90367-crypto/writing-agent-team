# Anti-Patterns

## 1. Repeating the whole story

**Bad**
A reply restates the original request, every prior action, all internal discussion, and then asks a one-line question.

**Fix**
Keep only the fact needed to bridge to the remaining ask.

## 2. Bureaucratic inflation

**Bad**
Use formal-document vocabulary in an ordinary email merely because the topic is administrative.

**Fix**
Use plain administrative Chinese unless the surface is actually an official document.

## 3. Softening until the rule disappears

**Bad**
A student deadline becomes "有空的話再麻煩盡快..." even when there is a hard cutoff.

**Fix**
State the actual deadline and consequence clearly, then keep the surrounding tone friendly.

## 4. Inventing future work

**Bad**
Add "後續我會再向您回報" when the user never said they plan to do that.

**Fix**
Stop after the actual next step.

## 5. Superficial multiple versions

**Bad**
Five options differ only by replacing "請問" with "想請問".

**Fix**
Vary structure: direct ask, context-first, collaborative framing, concise version, warmer version.

## 6. Accidental pressure

**Bad**
External scheduling language implies the other party should "配合" the user's preferred arrangement.

**Fix**
Ask availability/willingness and explain the coordination need neutrally.

## 7. Over-formal familiar communication

**Bad**
A LINE message to a familiar colleague reads like an official memo.

**Fix**
Keep the respect, remove ceremony, state the ask conversationally.

## 8. Over-apology

**Bad**
Several sentences apologize before a minor administrative correction.

**Fix**
Acknowledge the change once if needed, then state the correction and next step.
