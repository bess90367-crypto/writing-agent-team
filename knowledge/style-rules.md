# Durable Style Rules

## Core voice

The target voice is **natural, concise, complete, respectful, and administratively clear**.

The writing should sound like a competent human coordinator, not a template or regulation.

## Global rules

1. **Say the purpose early.** Do not spend a full paragraph on ceremony before the useful content.
2. **Give enough context, not all context.** Include only what the recipient needs to understand the current ask.
3. **One message should ideally finish one coordination cycle.** When practical, include background, current status, requested action, and deadline together so avoidable back-and-forth is reduced.
4. **Do not repeat known thread content.** In replies, summarize only the part necessary to bridge to the next step.
5. **Do not invent commitments.** Never add "I will report back", "I will follow up", "we will arrange..." unless the user said they intend to do it.
6. **Avoid bureaucratic inflation.** Prefer plain administrative Chinese over literary or overly official phrasing in ordinary email.
7. **Avoid accidental hierarchy.** Do not make the user sound like they are commanding a teacher/partner or excessively submitting to them.
8. **Be precise.** Preserve exact dates, times, amounts, document names, attachments, and institutional requirements.
9. **Do not soften away rules.** Friendly student-facing language must still state requirements and consequences clearly.
10. **Multiple versions must be meaningfully different.** Change structure, entry point, or interpersonal tone—not merely synonyms.

## Preferred email habits

- A common opening is: `收信平安！`
- For ordinary administrative emails, a simple name + `您好：` followed by `收信平安！` is often suitable.
- Closing language should be natural and useful. A common pattern is: `如有任何問題，歡迎與我聯繫，謝謝！`
- Do not mechanically force the same opening/closing into every short reply when it would feel redundant.

## Brevity

"簡潔" means:
- remove repeated explanation;
- shorten ceremony;
- merge logically connected sentences;
- keep the action request obvious.

It does **not** mean deleting the reason, deadline, or necessary administrative condition.

## Wording sensitivities

Prefer everyday accurate wording over inflated administrative synonyms. Example: when a phone call simply was not answered, say `沒有接通` rather than unnecessarily formal alternatives.

When coordinating externally, avoid wording that sounds like forcing the other party to `配合`, `讓出`, or `讓給` something when a neutral coordination phrase is available.
