# Scenario Rules

## 1. Follow-up without sounding like a催促

Use:
1. brief prior context;
2. current status or why the answer is now needed;
3. all remaining necessary question(s) or action(s), grouped clearly in the same message when practical.

Avoid:
- blame;
- repeated "尚未收到回覆";
- unnecessary urgency language when a date alone is enough.

## 2. Time-sensitive change

Acknowledge timing lightly when necessary, but do not over-apologize. Distinguish a late adjustment or replacement arrangement from admitting fault.

## 3. Requesting information

Explain **why the information is needed** when that makes the request more reasonable. Then list only the information the recipient must provide.

## 4. Asking someone to confirm a draft/process

State what has already been checked or completed, show the proposed wording/process, and ask whether anything else needs correction. This reduces multiple rounds.

## 5. Student rules and deadlines

Structure:
- what this concerns;
- what the student must do;
- exact deadline;
- consequence if applicable;
- where/how to respond.

## 6. External discrepancy or correction

Structure:
- neutral fact;
- mismatch/problem;
- why it matters administratively;
- requested confirmation/correction;
- next step after confirmation.

Do not frame the other party as careless unless the user explicitly needs responsibility stated.

## 7. Invitation / scheduling with external speaker

Ask availability or willingness without exposing unrelated people's schedules. Avoid pressure. If several dates are possible, ask which works; if none work, invite them to say so.

## 8. Event/vendor coordination

When another party asks whether students/staff should attend a meeting, first determine what they need those people to do or understand. Phrase this as willingness to coordinate plus a request for the intended discussion/participation purpose.

## 9. Administrative reply after internal confirmation

Once the school/internal unit has confirmed the answer, do not keep narrating the internal checking process. Tell the recipient the confirmed outcome and the next action.

## 10. Short public-facing copy

For posters/forms:
- one line if space is constrained;
- lead with benefit/limit/deadline;
- remove internal explanation;
- preserve any number or condition that changes behavior.

## 11. Internal date screening before a wider scheduling poll

When the user wants to check an internal team's constraints before polling a wider group, draft the internal check first. Ask whether the proposed date range works and whether any other dates should be excluded. Explicitly respect known unavailable dates; do not present them as available poll options or imply the wider poll has already been approved.

## 12. External honorarium / reimbursement information request

When an external partner's visit, lecture, supervision, or presentation has school-funded honorarium support but the actual payee is not yet known:

- first explain that funding was successfully obtained this term and, when relevant, frame it as a way to thank a long-term partner for supporting the program;
- state the amount per partner/unit and the intended eligible recipients (for example, the visit presenter or course supervisor) without deciding the payee on the partner's behalf;
- ask the partner to have the selected payee complete only the required/highlighted fields in the attached reimbursement form and return it;
- if the reimbursement form is currently split across multiple presenters but the partner may prefer one person to receive the full amount, explicitly invite them to say so and offer to adjust the reimbursement paperwork;
- make the action request clear while avoiding unnecessary explanation of internal accounting procedures.


## 13. Targeted internal compliance reminder with source notice

When a central administrative unit sends a lengthy training/compliance notice and the user has already checked which faculty or staff still need to complete a specific item:

- lead with the **specific outstanding item** and the **user-requested deadline**, including whether it is before close of business; do not make recipients search the forwarded notice to find the action;
- address the selected recipients without implying that every faculty member is noncompliant or that the user independently verified other requirements;
- if the user supplies practical course-selection guidance, incorporate it near the action request, then place the original central-unit notice below as reference, clearly separated from the user's own request;
- preserve exact course labels and deadlines. Do not confuse the original institution-wide compliance target with the user's narrower requested course, or invent a link, completion status, sanction, or deadline from the forwarded notice.

This is a targeted notice pattern, not a mandatory template for all faculty emails.
